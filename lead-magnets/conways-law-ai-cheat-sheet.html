Conway's Law for AI Cheat Sheet

Who should own which part of your AI system, and the team shapes that break it. One page.
AI Engineer Insights
aiengineerinsights.com · Nov 2026

1 · The law

"Any organization that designs a system (defined broadly) will produce a design whose structure is a copy of the organization's communication structure." Melvin Conway, 1968
  • In AI: every owner of a piece (model access, prompts, retrieval, evals, guardrails, tracing) becomes a seam in the system.
  • Inverse Conway maneuver: pick the target architecture first, then design teams that would naturally build it.
  • Evidence: org-structure metrics predicted failing Windows Vista binaries better than code metrics (Microsoft Research, 2008).

2 · Four team types, for AI

Stream-aligned (product)Owns the AI feature: prompts, tools, UX, its eval cases
PlatformGateway + routing, eval harness + CI gate, tracing, cost, guardrail enforcement, as a self-service
Complicated subsystemRetrieval/ranking at scale, fine-tuning, custom models
EnablingCoaches teams on prompts, evals, agent patterns, AI security, then steps away

3 · Who owns what

ComponentOwner
Model keys, access, routingAI platform

Eval harness, CI gateAI platform

RAG pipelineProduct team first; specialist team once shared
Guardrail policySecurity / risk, via enabling team
Guardrail enforcementAI platform, in the gateway
Tracing, cost dashboardsAI platform
Fine-tuning, custom modelsComplicated-subsystem team

4 · Interaction modes

CollaborationFirst agent or RAG feature, while both teams learn. Set an end date.
X-as-a-ServiceSteady state for gateway, evals, tracing, shared retrieval. No tickets needed.
FacilitatingEnabling team teaches eval writing, guardrails, agent patterns.
Most common failure: the platform team stays in collaboration with everyone, so it never becomes a service.

5 · Warning signs

  • Every AI feature waits in one central team's backlog.
  • Each team runs its own gateway, vector store and eval script.
  • The AI CoE writes guidelines nothing enforces.
  • Prompt or model changes ship with no eval gate.
  • You can't trace one request across two teams' services.
  • Agents have access everywhere, so they couple things no team agreed to couple.

6 · Setup by company size

Under ~30 engineersNo AI team. Product teams use a model API; one shared wrapper and eval script.
~30–150 engineersProduct teams + a thin AI platform team (2–4 people) for gateway, evals, tracing.
150+ engineersPlatform grouping, specialist teams for retrieval/models, an enabling team. "Hub and spoke."

7 · What an AI platform should publish

  • Models offered, with cost per call and latency targets
  • How to get access: self-service keys, no ticket
  • How to run evals and read results; the CI gate rules
  • Tracing format and where to find a request's trace
  • Guardrails enforced by default, and how to request exceptions
  • Owner, on-call and support channel
